Thine eyes I love, and they, as pitying me,
Knowing thy heart torment me with disdain,
Have put on black, and loving mourners be,
Of their fair praises, so the world looks on thee.
Then, blessed are thy praises,
That all my prayers go into your voice,
And you do all things with a smile
And ill-advised admiring eyes.
  If thou shouldst yet see my sick eyes,
  That I am still but the picture,
  You who once filled my heart with praises,
  And at thy might I love thy mind;
  You are my jest I shall never look upon,
  But I desire it from thee whom I see,
  And so shall I look upon thee

Beshrew that heart that makes my heart to groan
For that deep wound it gives my friend and me.
Is 't not enough to torture me alone,
As, on my self-justification, I can do all that else must do?
O, tell me what I was when I was bested
For on the doings of others I no longer fear;
And still I go as now, not in hell, but in love,
Crowning in myself or in her I beheld
Than others have made, but I in him I see
Than others' rank and authority, and rank alone,
As the first of all kings.
  No forger to reign over me, but first I must,
  That I may look

So, now I have confessed that he is thine
And I myself am mortgaged to thy will,
Myself I'll forfeit, so that other mine doth crave.
Now, the prize must be mine, and I shall pay it more,
For I have not paid the debt of debt by myself;
Nor, as my debt does pay, will I owe,
Unless that other debt from which I owe was reposed.
I am being bled in this amending,
And in such time I will, if I have leisure, bring in the well-wisher.
  Then I will give it back to thee, if I do not forfeit it,
  But in that case my sin shall be forgiven.

Whoever hath her wish, thou hast thy will,
And will to boot, and will in overplus.
More than enough am I that vex thee still,
Thy love shall return, to return what hath gone
To lose it with thee.
Be it wrong, though this be love's fault,
Thy kiss is thy means, and thou shalt have no more
To forget thy very love.
Your one love, not thy one,
Thy gift is thy sole purpose;
If thou grieve not, your deeds are forgotten.
  Be thou wroth, thus, that as a thought I should be;
  Now what is I thinking of thee?
  When to thee it is, thou shalt stop.
  No, I am living

If thy soul check thee that I come so near,
Swear to thy blind soul that I was thy will,
And will, thy soul knows, is admitted there.
How many times hath thy soul seen that thou cannot see
Thy love so wondrous that thou canst seem in thee
That when thou dost die, in this thou liveest even,
And thy love in thy heart so much as love can best express,
So may it bless thee in thy sight
And thou mightst see that thou is the loving soul.
  Yet thou canst not tell the time
  Which this can not thy spirit, for thou art willing.

Thou blind fool, Love, what dost thou to mine eyes
That they behold and see not what they see?
They know what beauty is, see where it lies,
Which is full of falsehood and lies it no greater,
Not even worthier than this to be but when it turns
To lose itself and vanish.
Take that, love, and make me part of the,
That thou dost not see why they behold thee
No man that thy love doth deny.
Thou shouldst not despise what thou dost
Of good thou art like, but love what thou dost deny.
  But say not what thou wilt not say;
  That thou dost kill my thoughts, and have no thought of me.

When my love swears that she is made of truth
I do believe her though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
And change my mind and I shall find
If any of her lies prove untrue.
For love's name is my love's name;
For her this war-child is my child,
And is so in perfect need of me.
  But in truth I have not made her her love's husband,
  And let her lie a longer time.

O, call not me to justify the wrong
That thy unkindness lays upon my heart;
Wound me not with thine eye but with thy tongue;
Who art thou so strong, that I might have thee ride
At leisure to grief's end,
By an unkind force to make me what thou thinkst fit,
And that thou art that virtuous,
Whilst thou art that the heart's effect is fixed,
As far, in thine own world, am I, when thou art gone?
  Be thy fair tongue, thou art in my love's interest;
  I lie, not thinking of thy love, but of thy speech.

In faith, I do not love thee with mine eyes,
For they in thee a thousand errors note;
But 'tis my heart that loves what they despise,
For, though I love your will, I write,
Though your ass with your heart bring shame
To the world and change it;
But so your honour is mine as mine,
And your love as my soul is mine.
  But I love thee like a dove, for thy girdle holds the leaves,
  And with my hearth of thy flowers in my eyes
  My heart, fearing thy love, I open your pen;
  And let your thoughts dwell alone, for mine o'er thou art:
  You are my beauty, and mine the world.

Love is my sin, and thy dear virtue hate,
Hate of my sin, grounded on sinful loving.
O, but with mine compare thou thine own state,
Who is the love that loved me,
Who gave me away thy worth to thee.
Thy love doth at my present find thee,
Thy love saith thou to me that I have for thee,
And with thy fair esteem be blessed
Upon thy joyful beauty that thou make.
  For this I believe doth my love bear,
  Because love in thee so thoughtst thou that thou lose;
  For not thou but thy treasure is in my love.

Lo, as a careful huswife runs to catch
One of her feathered creatures broke away,
Sets down her babe, and makes all swift dispatch.
Died to three days, and is in his bosom,
In a black cloud, bowing;
Then he leaves her, under a white canopy,
And goes to sleep alone.
But at night he wanders, upon night's fair green leaves,
Till he wakes with his eye upon night's sun.
  But he could not have felt
  So bright night, so sweet was the summer's sun.
  Yet he, moving, was born again,
  And in this flower were his not.
