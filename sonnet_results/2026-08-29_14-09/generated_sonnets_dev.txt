Knowing thy heart torment me with disdain,
Have put on black, and loving mourners be,
To grant me new fare.
  But if thou art the most fair, I am the most fair;

  And when thou reaps thee thou art born again.
  But my hart was great when I was born,
  And, though dead, thou art for love beautiful now,
  And henceforth thou shalt have long life.

So, now I have confessed that he is thine
And I myself am mortgaged to thy will,
Myself I'll forfeit, so that other mine thou lov'st.
Thy pleasure is to my heart more than my worth;
My pleasure is more than his value;
Thy pleasure is more than mine but thy worth;
My pleasure is far better than his other valor;
Then how are you so ill in heart that you are glad of it?
  How are you happy in your eyes
  When you live? Are your eyes glad enough to see that I am wretched?
  Then you will leave me, and leave not the love that I have to share
  With the ghosts of your pleasure, your pleasures are not mine.

Whoever hath her wish, thou hast thy will,
And will to boot, and will in overplus.
More than enough am I that vex thee still,
Than thou hadst me to draw this thy humor.
But to thee is my love made,
To thee is the love of life done,
And the wonder of life's taming thee mine.
O, why hast thou not wilt I now please
And by my counsel, yet suffer thy live,
Or else he is to thee nothing but an everlastingly loyal guest?

Thou blind fool, Love, what dost thou to mine eyes
That they behold and see not what they see?
They know what beauty is, see where it lies,
And what kind of a memory they can treasure
Against what they have heard.
But that which thou art, thou dost not believe,
And art not what they believe.
A loveliness that they have seen but not what they have
Descends from the darkness of the mind,
And verily is thyself, though thou hast not seen it.
  My heart is still tender for love,
  But my eyes were deaf to the remembrance of the beauty,
  And I still am as blind unto love.

When my love swears that she is made of truth
I do believe her though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
And any other kind of fool that might disdain.
But I shall never find her evil like herself,
Nor fear to be the cause of her's undoing.
Be true, and with her thou shalt be like unto her,
And know thee well for thou art thy own fortune.
  What wisdom do I think thou art, if thou art like to fall.

O, call not me to justify the wrong
That thy unkindness lays upon my heart;
Wound me not with thine eye but with thy tongue;
While thou art neglecting me I shall be far from thy anger,
The truth that thou knowest but do not live.
Thou art not my friend, but I shall soon lose thee.
But this love between us I cannot break,
Nor in thee ever shall be returned,
Nor in thee will I find fault.
  All must be yet for thee to find out,
  But let this excuse then be thy right,
  And then do the better, for I will never do evil.

Be wise as thou art cruel; do not press
My tongue-tied patience with too much disdain,
Lest sorrow lend me words, and words express my thoughts.
Now, if thou hast heard well, tell me of thy woe;
Thou art a coward in all wisdom; though evil
For sorrow, thou art in thy folly alone;
Or with reason, you may do well by keeping
Beside me, who yet, though good, still speak true.
  My judgment brings me this correction,
  And your heart to love me too full when you kiss my hand.

In faith, I do not love thee with mine eyes,
For they in thee a thousand errors note;
But 'tis my heart that loves what they despise,
So that thou dost set my love on thine own;
Thou art my best love, and thy brightest doth reward.
Thou art fair, fair before me,
The love of me which neither I nor thou dost love.
For thou art my love of war, and therefore,
Love of my nature is my love, and thou art my lover's.
  Thou art my love, thy love that thou dost take,
  That thou didst love to be loved by all.

10

Love is my sin, and thy dear virtue hate,
Hate of my sin, grounded on sinful loving.
O, but with mine compare thou thine own state,
Mine over-against thy gain and gain against mine,
With mine, thou liest, with mine thou glory,
With mine, thou risest, with mine thou stumbles;
Therefore love, when in thee thou art true,
Thou art thy own soul, thy own sin.
    Therefore love in thee is false beauty.
