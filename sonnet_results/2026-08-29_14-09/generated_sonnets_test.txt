Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
And although I lived in despair,
I felt that every hour that I lived
Sank down in love, And still had God's peace in me.
  So that even though I lived so,
  I could never seem to know love,
  That nor nor life ever was me more.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
For to the king's eye thou dost seem,
Whose eye is the eye of thy beauty,
But whose eyes to the golden eye thou suffer'st?
O, what mayst thou by thy own strength gain,
That by her to thy soul mayst be born?
  Thou wilt bear an unfulfilled portion,

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
That is called the womb of the ill,
And my love is an ill-domed rose
Feeding in that same place where my sorrow
Was born, where I laid myself the sad wretches.
  But you are the self-same, a day still dies,
  That I love you most.
  Then love doth live in me long after my death,
  And sorrow doth die in my flesh before my death.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Which knows nothing but the pursuit of eyes?
My heart is neither in trust nor in leisure
Of such subjects as can serve my eyes.
If I do not heed not the rules,
Since thou art right, do I not say so
Nor think so, for thou art right too.
  I live only by thy laws, and yet they are the most
  essential laws,
  For either my mind is cast away by them,
  Or my affections deprive me of this sight.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
For my heart is too young to know how
When we shall both be long buried.
An old lover shall now be crowned,
And a young lover shall be despised,
If ever we shall live two lives.
   Be more sweet in your lips than our hearts are weak;
  For time will pity us no more,
  And time shall judge that we were both born.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn;
That thou shalt not both kiss me, yet kiss me not.
Behold, my love, his love hath not found thee yet;
Nor hath he none cause to love me, but he doth love me not.
But do that which thou do'st make him love thee.
Thou art not dead, and he thy dear steed;
He is but thou dost love him.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
For whom no authority lies,
But only is the proud mother of my love.
  And I do believe she, but, though I be true,
  I am not so strong as my love,
  Nor yet my worth to thee.
  She loves me, she loves my youth,
  And her love is my love.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And gladly may I boast him my best,
That he always have some office or reason
To say, this grace with mine was better,
In serving any brotherhood than this to sit
So well among my fellow-titans.
O, in thee I see love's beauty even in thy shade,
And in thee in mine every color doth shine;
That love's beauty in thee, far more than mine
Is greater than any other friend can teach.
  Thou art my true creation, and in thee, my love
  Love cannot die, for he is my very wisest.
