Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
When that love should wakened, she alone
Could be my sweet friend's muse,
Whose sweet air, though new, sounds as old
As ever the breast of Time's most gracious queen.
O, give me not Thy burden of tears, my sweet muse,
Or I would not sing at all;
Be thou my sweet friend, but my tongue
Be not confounded by that tongue that loves thee.
  Be not bound with Thy foul lip to sing what belongs
  To the tongue of old, which taketh away.

When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot thee
Or laid him to rest, in the midst of sorrow?
To be thou, then, in my love is in me all the woe

O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
Or tell false to any contrary proof;
Thy right to be praised, and thy falsehood shame,
As well in me as in thy sovereign.
Make haste, then, to repent, and waken me,
So that I may not be blamed so soon
As thou art to blame, that I deceive
Thy gracious deare.
O, what wilt thou by my living hope do,
That I may not be guilty of being false?
  Therefore believe thou not that I live,
  But only that thou mayst remain by my side.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep,
But the lovely flame of beauty did still burn.
For beauty lives in fire, and in that
Beauty knows not what it's to do.
But this woman, whose name was Diana,
Was possessed with hot passion by her lover's will,
And being for joy like to the sun
Picked him up in her closet and laid him down.
So that her beauty was almost beside,
And yet not in dead wood did he grow,
But within her lay an antique precious:
  This maid of the utmost beauty that ever lived.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
And then I must flee to my state,
To my happy separation, in love with another.
But no; I cannot depart
Nor cross the sea nor begin that quest
Which may make me repent of my woe,
To seek of love's dearest aid another,
But I leave my beloved alone, and bring her home.
  Then love's strength to me shall be thine,
  And I in that state shall praise thee more.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Who, without wit, so delivers my love
From cold to heat, sweet to bitter, cold to hot.
But that sweet God, for my sake, what shall I call
His love? I do not know; love is none other
Than that which hath lodged in my bosom.
No, I do not call it love for all that it is;
My love is my right trust, and not his fair virtue.
  Let us then in this be silent, and say,
  'Here I am, there I am, both in the same right.'
