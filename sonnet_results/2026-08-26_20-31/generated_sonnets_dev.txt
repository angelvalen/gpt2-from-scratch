Whoever hath her wish, thou hast thy will,
And will to boot, and will in overplus.
More than enough am I that vex thee still,
Thy power stronger than mine is either weak or strong;
And though no man's wit can hold thee back,
I may still swear by thy power, though not be true;
For thou, being thy power, is my best trust,
And my worst foe, a mortal foe to be hated,
Like other men can I reckon but by name.
  Thus thou art my muse, my poet, my muse's rhyme,
  And that is so true that none can prove the contrary.

Thou blind fool, Love, what dost thou to mine eyes
That they behold and see not what they see?
They know what beauty is, see where it lies,
And what is blessed, what is hated, what is well loved,
What is forbidden, what is unlooked for,
What is dear, what is despised, what is dear's use;
So, to thy blind eyes, mine own eyes, by all looks,
Are full of thy beauty, what I in thee know,
And mine own eye to thee is thy treasure.
  To treasure this treasure do I fight,
  But thee alone I fight for treasure, thou alone my foe.

When my love swears that she is made of truth
I do believe her though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Who should not learn all my invention
That leads to truth in his all-conquering pen,
Making with his cunning such pen as that taught
The poor, the sick, and the poor of the world.
So I do believe her, though I know she is a lie;
  For that she might see the beauty of my skill,
  If that truth should come to me by fair means.

Be wise as thou art cruel; do not press
My tongue-tied patience with too much disdain,
Lest sorrow lend me words, and words express too much pain.
Be not too tender when my love holds thee fast,
Or in my no tender content a son may depart;
Be not so rude as to be cruel
When I love with thee I fear to depart,
But let every injury be forgotten,
And let my love wane while I live, that we may
Die with each other's presence assured.
  Make every single task of my will,
  Which is more than sorrow and more than pain,
  Whose salve is every woe and every injury.

In faith, I do not love thee with mine eyes,
For they in thee a thousand errors note;
But 'tis my heart that loves what they despise,
And therein I see thy beauty's double glory,
So that 'twixt two dost thou do adore.
For 'tis in 'gainst thee all that doth the mind
Which shapes the stars, and gives thy youth life,
And lends not to fortune but lends thine treasure.
  But although thou be many a shadow,
  Yet in thine eyes my love, strong as a tiger, shines bright.
