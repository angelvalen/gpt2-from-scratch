Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
I should have liked her true love, not her dearest love
That from thee in whose name I love bears:
But to thee, from thee alone my love grows,
And to thee to me these are my favorites;
As for thee, from my side thou seem'st to die.
  And by the first love that I love am dead,
  When love's last death I see thee treading,
  And for thy sake me may live when thou art dead.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When in thy great being I look upon thee?
A spirit hath made thee to hold and draw back
For which my sufferings and loss may be show'd;
But, poor soul, even that was still alive
Would suffer my body more than thou didst feel,
If not if thou couldst keep myself together.
O wretched you, poor soul, even in thy dying,
Thy sorrow and my pain are equal,
Nor their less equal in distance.
  So to love the heaven in thee I show thee my face
  And tell how my world is in thy own breast.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
In luxury's mind with rare gifts which I
Can never hope to give,
But whose past life shall ever lie clear
Unto heaven's gloom; who doth live
By sweet fare worth the cold of winter,
And enjoys a solech of the air;
And now his beauty I love and hate,
And hate that love wherewith you wish
To subdue me with your eyes.
My little love is sweet, and dreads me,
So I love him to him and hate him to me;
  And when I am hated he cares not,
  Nor can I hate him
