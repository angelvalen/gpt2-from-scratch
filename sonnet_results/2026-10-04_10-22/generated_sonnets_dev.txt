That she might think me some untutored youth,

In thy children's names, while I deny they are;

Love is my sin, and thy dear virtue hate,
Hate of my sin, grounded on sinful loving.
O, but with mine compare thou thine own state,
The true reason of thy dwelling in my child,
And how thou, with me this act of mine,
Doth test myself to rest on thee.
Be Thou no more without me in this misery,
But do this in my presence, so that this time I do not hate thee,
But thee alone; for thy love hath more than these faults.
  If I were thy wife I should pray thee to let me die,
  That if thy love troubled me I should be happy.

Lo, as a careful huswife runs to catch
One of her feathered creatures broke away,
Sets down her babe, and makes all swift dispatch
When she catches another as flight follows,
To save her which fell by the bow;
But when she does find another, she breaks it in twain
To stay the limb that escapes her;
And what can she do, unless it be her self,
Or her nature, if it be nature's self?
Thus, in her self-less love of others' ill,
She passes her hands in the strongest hand,
Which sees not their grace, and that they may be
Of no account in heaven save that which the world has.
