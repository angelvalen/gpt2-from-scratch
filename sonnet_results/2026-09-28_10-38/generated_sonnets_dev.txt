Thine eyes I love, and they, as pitying me,
Knowing thy heart torment me with disdain,
Have put on black, and loving mourners be,
And you in heaven where I am buried,
Have breath in my spirit, and bring me back again.
Therefore thou hast power to this effect,
Which alone can take away sorrow from me.
But I trust my love, whom I like to see,
The more, in seeing thy sweet face, for fear
That thou by this action deny
The rights of thy sweet mind, that thy sight
O'erswept me from remembrance.
  So, though my grief be heavy, my sorrow no less,
  Since thou, my love, is my friend.

Whoever hath her wish, thou hast thy will,
And will to boot, and will in overplus.
More than enough am I that vex thee still,
Than more, in truth, than the birds of the air sing.
The fire, my love, I beseech thee, burneth
For a gilded coffin in thee,
Which to my loving mother, buried in thy own,
She takes to call the loved one home.
Thou wilt be wont to mourn no more then what's the matter
With artless parts, and in sweet form shall it go.
  But I know that sorrow with thee doth not fail,
  Where thou art hidden, where thou art buried.

Be wise as thou art cruel; do not press
My tongue-tied patience with too much disdain,
Lest sorrow lend me words, and words express me wrong.
O, but be still, if thou art in despair,
Thy gentle self mayst be dead with ease.
Hate's worst foe to the fair is to groan,
And to love his pride bewails his love.
Thou art the fairest in beauty that doth compare,
And thy beauty doth use all thou hast.
  Woe to me if I should then add this,
  Lest being too proud should make love dull;
  To love is the full measure of goodness.

In faith, I do not love thee with mine eyes,
For they in thee a thousand errors note;
But 'tis my heart that loves what they despise,
And I, thy meek love, in spite of myself
Doth bear no wrong to one who hath heard me writ.
For though thy plain anger do tend to make me tell
That this poor love is less than thou,
Thou art my creation, and must needs be mine.
Therefore love me not with thy eyes, which see thine;
I love thee with mine own heart, and hate not thy eyes
Which I make in thee out of thy worthless treasure.
  And it is better that this should seem than to do
  To make thee love me more than my worth.

Love is my sin, and thy dear virtue hate,
Hate of my sin, grounded on sinful loving.
O, but with mine compare thou thine own state,
Thy own comfort, thy own excuse,
And, knowing me, believe thyself right.
Therefore, being late, I hate thee,
And hate thee but in the shadows of time,
Or dost thou myself see, in thee my love?
  I do not care that thou me should be the main,
  I care that thy goodness may see my matter,
  But in thee alone I find my fault, and therefore.
