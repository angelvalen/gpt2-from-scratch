Thine eyes I love, and they, as pitying me,
Knowing thy heart torment me with disdain,
Have put on black, and loving mourners be,
Stingtingting me with unkind cries.
As I weep, they reproach me with toil;
So are their mouths, as if they bore it out;
And my tongue, beseeching 'gainst them, loveth their accents.
  Yet I love them more than thou art, nay,
  Thine eyes more than they themselves would bear,
  For mine are the sweetest eyes in heaven.

Beshrew that heart that makes my heart to groan
For that deep wound it gives my friend and me.
Is 't not enough to torture me alone,
To be told, 'gainst my trust, of thy love;
To hear the sweetest love expressed in woe
Upon that doom which hath bound thee so fast?
O, forget me not, poor heart, for thou dost win
The conquest of love's queen; but return,
For thy friend's sake and mine own,
And in the doom that doth ensue with thee I'll leave.
  But if this forfeit of thee do not make thee lose,
  Give me leave to weep, though out of thy treasure.  Then truly I do love thee more.

Thou blind fool, Love, what dost thou to mine eyes
That they behold and see not what they see?
They know what beauty is, see where it lies,
And what shadows it best conceals;
They know how men and women look, what hue their eyes,
How fair and dark their faces, and what bright veins do grow
Upon the wrinkles of their brows.
Thou blind fool, Love, what do I to mine eyes
That they see what thou dost dost see?
They know what it is which I in thee behold,
Which maketh and shaketh the heart, which doth give life,
And what signs it gives to all that love it.
  Love knows all this, what it doth show thee,

When my love swears that she is made of truth
I do believe her though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
As in my breast she encloses
The heart of love and all the strength of woman.
But, nay, such a faith as mine is,
She loves not the sight of wrinkles, nor suffers wrinkles
To be seen in my bosom, lest she deceive me.
Nor can wrinkles, in her dear friend's breast,
Inherit their beauty, though she devise a vase
With cherubins and rainbows of white and roses of red.
  Thus, if my love be false, she hath my trust,
  But if she be true, I am her deceiver.

O, call not me to justify the wrong
That thy unkindness lays upon my heart;
Wound me not with thine eye but with thy tongue;
Make me unlive by thy unworthiness,
And all my life for thy sake must be
Lived in hatred, and sorrows like this
Of many a love-betrayed lover to be,
Without any remedy or means to be cured.
O, stop my tears, methinks,
For thy part is such a barren thing
That they will weep and weep and weep even more,
Bearing but thy gentle pride in their eyes;
  If thyself to be be wakened, cry not;
  The sweetest tears are sweetest graces.

In faith, I do not love thee with mine eyes,
For they in thee a thousand errors note;
But 'tis my heart that loves what they despise,
And though thou despisest what I do, yet for love of me
Thou art thy sovereign love, and I do approve.
Thence I date thy life and have you all teached,
Although thy birth be but such a rare fool
As not to live nor die can be taught.
  To know thee I have read all the poet's lines,
  And after myself I have profaned thine antiquity;
  But 'tis most pleasing to me that thou be my witness,
  Which in the eyes of so many a throng
  I do esteem my greatest friend.
