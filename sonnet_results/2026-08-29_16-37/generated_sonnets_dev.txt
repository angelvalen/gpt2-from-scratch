Thine eyes I love, and they, as pitying me,
Knowing thy heart torment me with disdain,
Have put on black, and loving mourners be,
To purify my hue with sepulchers unseen.
O, that they should suffer me to lie sickened
In their shade with unfeigned tears, to be sad,
And at their feeble assistance confound
My sweet self, thou who hast in me such love,
And to a tyrant my public dignities!
  O, that they should in themselves confound me
  By making me their tyrant! And what shall I do
  To be their tyrant, but to obey
  The will of Thee, and do nothing in return?

Thou blind fool, Love, what dost thou to mine eyes
That they behold and see not what they see?
They know what beauty is, see where it lies,
And so to my eyes do they gaze in gazing frown.
Look how thou hast wasted my treasure,
And now mine eye doth witness the day,
And on my bosom there do hang a grudge
That was mine, whether mine heart or mine eye.
  'Tis mine eye, and yet this is my mind.
  'Tis mine eye, and yet this doth witness the day.

When my love swears that she is made of truth
I do believe her though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Who knows not how well he may deceive;
I know that I cannot do good, or cannot be evil,
Unless my love should first give her that witness
That I love her with that love which proves
By her judgment, and what it proves,
The great difference between one myself and another.
  Then do I believe my love to be deceived,
  And if she is deceived she knows it, and says so,
  And if my love is deceived, it is my will to be so.

O, call not me to justify the wrong
That thy unkindness lays upon my heart;
Wound me not with thine eye but with thy tongue;
For where thine eyes are, mine eyes are always wide;
Thy one gift, that my nature with thee doth crave
And to my dull sight teacheth to read my thoughts,
Thy hue and thy beauty doth confound me.
For in thee my sight doth read all my heart,
And mine eyes do learn thine image.
  So that thy sight, so that mine eye, doth see
  All the beauty of mine affections.

8

Be wise as thou art cruel; do not press
My tongue-tied patience with too much disdain,
Lest sorrow lend me words, and words express thy grief.
I weep and ask for my days are done; yet still my plea
Is not enough to please thee, and my love unfrequented.
  Thou art my life, my delight, my sweetest delight;
  I crave thy aid in my weakness, and this should be
  As well as thine: be patient, sweet heart, and then be done.

9

In faith, I do not love thee with mine eyes,
For they in thee a thousand errors note;
But 'tis my heart that loves what they despise,
And in their sole substance is love.
O, in the things in me thou dost find grace,
Which thou givest me and know'st not how,
And I dwell therein on thee alone,
With eyes but as stork do in the air.
Then my love, though it be blind, shall be true,
And have eyes like that which have eyes to look
With whom each thing stands so rank;
  Since my heart is mine eye, what else but heart doth it see
  But thy heart, my love, which in thy sight dost behold.
