My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
And tormenting that which doth break the body.
I love not whose cure is feeble,
Nor that which is broken in parts;
No, in short, I am nothing but love itself.
O, if love would weep, that it would let me know,
For love would be my mistress, and my mistress be my love,
And love my love with more love and less loss.
  But love cannot weep for its loss, for it is nothing;
  For how could it weep if it did not survive?

Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot thee,
And on the false glory in that forgetfulness
Make thee a pleasure so strong that I cannot go
Without desire for thy love, till I see thee buried?
Then the earth which thou dost make will feel sour
And will show how much pleasure doth this art afford.
For me love cannot so lively renew,
Since mine own dooth die, and in thee no life lives.
  Therefore I love thee so far, though yet I know it lies.

O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
Thy excuse, my weak self doth blame me;
I am thy slave, and, if thou wilt choose, love me still.
'Tis not that thou hast power to deceive
Nor that I know anything art in thee,
But only that thou knowsest my weakness and is willing
To win me, if thou wilt, by thy virtue win me.
  Yet this my love is nothing, and therefore love's desire

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Or I may lead you on too far; yet I will leave
Thy guilty love, or give thee a sweet waste
And lead thee of your love's habitation's way.
Therefore, leave me, sweet love, till that time
When words can but lamely express my will,
Making you a show of shame and the world
Of false kings and false fools.
  For time will not forget thy wrong, but love will mend it,
  And should your sweet love still walk the crooked course
  When words cannot express the situation, I leave you still.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
And fear in thy self-love I shun.
Love, whose spite so sweetly didst amaze,
Doth, as a beggar doth his tattered garments,
The same in thy subject give.
So thou know'st, on me as in a beggar,
The hate of a self-love bestow'd.
  Such is love's character that despite foes and foes'
  Enemies, despite all those mighty arms,
  The rage of such rage is still, and ever withers.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
That through love's admiring glance I will stand
In my youth, perhaps, but least among men.
She doth best, that is, I do think she doth deceive,
For she hears what she thinks and doth desire me to say,
The contrary of truth and of those truths which thou dost dost give me.
  She needs not such books as might give her lie,
  For it is better she doth deceive me than she doth tell.
