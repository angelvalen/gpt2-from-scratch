Thine eyes I love, and they, as pitying me,
Knowing thy heart torment me with disdain,
Have put on black, and loving mourners be,
And now my eye no longer with pain behold
Thy lovely face, but do strange speak
To tell me, and thou wilt not in truth tell.
Hence arise I that we may hate these
Who set me forth to serve thee, and I
In going forth to thee may think thee a wretch.
Therefore, sir, love me not with thy thoughts,
But mine eyes, which shall tell thee this.

In thy beauty's sweetness, so that I lose
Myself for thy visage's sake, and lose

Lo, as a careful huswife runs to catch
One of her feathered creatures broke away,
Sets down her babe, and makes all swift dispatch.
Her sorrow is for that broken hope in the womb,
That thus doth she weave a plan and set her home;
And when she finds it lost, finds yet another.
So is it in a stranger's heart, that while he lies
The child is delivered, and all is right.
If there be reason to wonder, what if the child be dead?
