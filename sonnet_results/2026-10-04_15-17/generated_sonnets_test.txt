My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
And nurseth it more upon that which doth preserve it.
I see the love of the sweet doth bleed
In that which doth preserve it better,
That doth lend it to his old health,
And betimes doth keep it in his youth.
I see the sweet doth in youth lose
That which doth preserve him for his age,
And that doth lose it all his age,
Losing it to a child whose time he loves.
  His time is his last, and the love of him doth live.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn;
And thou, I pray, from that time never shall
A new deed of love declare.
Thy love, while true, is true, and no man has
The greatest love'st in him that loves thee.
And then from thy heart thou art that love'st,
Thy own self doth love thee all of thine.
I love thee, therefore, that thou may'st do so,
And in thine own self I love thee all,
The soul of love'st in thee.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep,
And as his limbs consumed, he breathed his last.
As the day, still alive, sleeps, so the night is dead;
But, as well as in days, when the sun is black,
His life is a star born. I lay down my head,
My love lay upon my lips,
And from his death, the days rolled away,
And the moon, as she lay, brought me home.
  The drowsy maid, after that, died for love.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Who oft and oft doth wonder at a thing,
With this to be certain he hath forethought.
The lover in love is very false,
For if his nature be true,
It must be so, for nothing lies but truth.
So, my love, though you should see me
Yourself, you will not see me.
  Love is more thy friend than the world is.
