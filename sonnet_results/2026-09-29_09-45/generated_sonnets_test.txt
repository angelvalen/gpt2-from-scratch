Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Carrying all things on thy back with thee?
Thou art my queen, to whom I go to roam

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
And with a fearful grim prognosis
Maketh the sickness dreaded by the brave.
When that fever passes away, my love's mind
Is changed to love, and in that love all is done.
My love doth not shake when the spirit of love
Doth pass from my breast, but my love's thoughts are changed;
Then I know that all my love's good goes,
And my sorrows are ended; so I stay in love.
  But if that love's life should end, my sorrows end.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Which I, having seen, can retort by reason?
O, no; for in my straight view is my view
Drawn from thy most dear face, which in thy goings is seen.
Yet, by my love's subjecting, I see thee,
And dost thence give that great help to see
Which should only mine own skill, my own eyes, prove.
  To be ignorant of thy form doth be mine sin,
  Which when love's rule has been learned I understand.

Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot thee?
Or do I think that thou, with thy tattered pride,
Strikes my sweet cheeks with thine own scars?

O, give me thy bloody self's blood, then let me stay,

  Or do thou in my love's praise add further,
  To me or thy sweet love's praise, both thine and mine.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Making me thy paramour, and make me swear
That thou wilt what thou wilt, nor mist'ry think,
Nor have thyself confounded with my faults,
Nor bethink me what I owe thee, nor faine
To go begging what I dare to beg.
If thou wilt, thy heart is mine, and I am thine.
If thou wilt not, thou wilt be my traitor,
And mine, being thine, will for a love's name live.
  Tis to make others good that thou make thine.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn;
In thought her love sealed her father's hate;
In love her suppliant is his prison-keeper,
And love in his chain his prey to prey.
Thou art twice so forsworn, that, being wed,
I know thee so still to be in love,
Yet thy feigning my love thou know'st not,
Nor does my love know it but by the look.
  Whate'er thou know'st, thou behold'st my love,
  And my love in thy face doth speak and tell.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep,
And when the holy ember did scorch,
Sweet Dian's maid, with tears and with tears
Of blessed knowledge lay dead.
The reverend maid had her head-dress made of gold,
And upon it was fastened a gold ring,
And upon that was all beauty in heaven.
Then her beauty with tears did besprinkle,
And yet not so rich as the sun,
Which so first and more richly in the night
Resembled the holy ember which lay there.
  So, sweet Dian, she lay, and her kind eye grew old.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Whose skin was fat with mother's milk, her fingers cozened,
And have of old himself made a hero.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
To whom I tremble and am a prey to fear;
And yet if the world I love conquer I fear him,
A very traitor to my soul's true woe.
Such loves are a prison to me, being
Wherein I am most surcharged, most vexed.
My more heavenly love, that can neither see
Nor do any wail on my behalf,
Shall be my constant prisoner, in the pit.
  Why should I leave you then, sweet love, if you love me?
  I will leave you only for your sake.
