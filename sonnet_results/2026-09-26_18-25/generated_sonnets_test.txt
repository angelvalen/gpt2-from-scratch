Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
When thou dost not know what love is
To be thy slave, but where thy heart must be,

Which woe be gracious, the lack of hope,

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn.
Worse, thou art now too old to appear
A dear with thee to my heart,
Even as the first love's joy never returned.
Told thou were old, I did not weep,
For thou art all my love's treasure.
Thy love makes a child's treasure and stills old;
O, how many a rose must thy breath leap?
Thy love shall not fill mine sorrow,
Or make any other such argument.
  No, thou art bound by thee, my dear,
  To be my chaste sweet only if thou are not dead.
