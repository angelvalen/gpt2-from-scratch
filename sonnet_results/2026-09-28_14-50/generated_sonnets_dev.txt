Thine eyes I love, and they, as pitying me,
Knowing thy heart torment me with disdain,
Have put on black, and loving mourners be,
And this in me alone would be my sorrow:
Thus thou, looking on mine eyes, look'st so;
But as thy love's blackness doth thy gaze betray,
So hath mine in me looked at with envy.
And thence thou that lovesst mine eyes doth love mine heart,
But to him that me looks so doth love my sight,
Thou, my sweet self, my blacked eyes,
Since mine eyes are black, are mine inward beauteous joy.
  This love then is not mine, nor in me it is.

One writes verse and the other word writs verse.
  I, untutored youth, though I seem blest
  To my fair muse, in spite of thee I suffer;
  Therefore, my love lives on.

In faith, I do not love thee with mine eyes,
For they in thee a thousand errors note;
But 'tis my heart that loves what they despise,
And I love thy most humble soul more
Than all else that thou art most of all.
Thy very nature, indeed, is no part petty,
And is but thy single self, pleasing and sweet,
Of whom that thou art'st the most true.
Thy precious children, for their praise are but children
Which love to thee, and sing with thee they sing,
That thou in them to myself mayst present.
  But thou, for beauty of heart, why art I more of thee
  Than all that is of the world, or every thing I make?

Love is my sin, and thy dear virtue hate,
Hate of my sin, grounded on sinful loving.
O, but with mine compare thou thine own state,
So my dear-hearted love my love is, and,
Since thou art mine, mine heart and mine action are in the same;
O, but thou art so made that thou art my love.
O, but love is of thy pride and not of mine,
As in a house where thou dost dwell,
That all are in thee, and my poor child,
As in thine own hand doth my good pleasure stand.
  But methinks my true love doth live in thee,
  Wherefore thou art my pleasure and mine own heart.
