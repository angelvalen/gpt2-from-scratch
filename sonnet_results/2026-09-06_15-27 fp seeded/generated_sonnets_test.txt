Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
A little younger tongue than mine that sang
The words to those lips that in my tongue told
A world of love had found in vain,
And thought the world a precious bargain,
From whence he ceased to write.
  No woman's tongue bore that song to heart.
  As I have the love of the world, it is mine;
  As I have the tongue that loves it, it is mine.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Thy own pride, in opposition to the seasons,
Than I increase my pride by thy love,
And from thee let my love return to thee.
Sweet heart, these eyes of mine behold
Upon thee the days that I died and thy present world,
Which should make thee comforted
When thou shouldst see me happy and proud.
  But thou, blessed be thy love, love's time of rejoicing,
  And I to thee not allow my very desire to die.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Feeding on that which doth bring it to death.
So I, fearful of my coming doom,
Thy love dull and thy face obscure;
My truth spent in tempests, my love unprovided.
For my love, by a strange wonder of mind
I can not make it thy friend's foe.
But perhaps what thy boldness does best,
Is to profit thee in all thy errors.
  Mine eye is my treasure, thy eye is mine part.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
A woman so fair, so pretty, so free,
And so sweet that he despised might be,

To give love to a woman dear
He thought was so poor as he could steal.
  But such, so sweet a mistress, doth love linger
  Save in the warmth of his bosom, in the sweet-smelling cup.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
That I cannot, even now, teach myself in books.
For that her heart doth have time's grace to delight,
And that I can use it then for a scribe's lesson;
But in my thinking, as in her, doth she make me behold.
  She hath love of truth made of heat,
  That hath many a learned friend to her,
  That love doth teach me many a thing,
  And many a new thing hath been told me.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
That better I make my home for that helps me;
My more poor sinner is a poor example,
That better he makes me teach
That, if my faults were so strong, he should yield
To the imperfection of me.
  But good and evil I love so well,
  That, if one be my friend and another my foe,
  The one of us be my friend, the other my foe.
