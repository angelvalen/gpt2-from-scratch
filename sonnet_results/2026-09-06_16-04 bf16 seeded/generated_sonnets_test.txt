Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Without sinning for me, knowing how I die?
O, let me hear thee speak; for what can compare
To the sweetest music of thy pen,
Which in thy fine spirit doth writ so fair a verse?
O, hear how I find fault with thee,
And in what on mine eyes do complain
Of thy coward folly, much greater than myself.
  For since thou didst take my sweet, sweet love away
  Since then, till this present day, I suffer no change,
  So high a pitch of anger in thee or I.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
And making me sick for that which heals me.
Therefore, as fever, I do much waste
For love of that which would nourish my wretch,
And after myself come to nought
But fever itself. Yet am I most wretched
By my adulteration of that which I love,
Whilst as if I was dead, death is my pleasure.
Therefore, being so, I do nothing of worth,
Nor live for myself, unless I be thought worthy.
  I am quite gone, being self-love,
  Lovers are but sick and their love to live.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
When, having knowledge of other's thoughts,
With mine own is my part hidden?
O, if it be true, then my judgment so deceived,
As mine own eyes cannot see the truth,
To a lie be made an eye for my sake;
For where my thoughts have eyes, they can see well,
And, having eyes, judgment of deeds doth much lie.
  To that case, what difference hath that I in thee
  But this, that you, being all one, are all one?

Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot thee,
And think I sin, when in thee I am not wroth?
If I hate thee in that state, do I not hate myself,
When I hate myself, hate myself in doing so?
Or else, by love, can I love myself to be so bad
That I be hated for it by others?
O, forbid me such a thought, and I will not give way,
Nor think on myself so cruel a thing.
  And in thee do I see love, and in thee I find many
  All sweet in themselves and in others.

O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
To make me look upon a false love,
To play the fool on thee, to know thee false,
And to this fashion show myself false to thee

O, what gentle change can thy shadow make
By shadowing thee in every hue,
Making my true vision appear false,
Making me for myself your fool?
  If my true sight to thee would appear
  Were shadowed, so as I would not look upon thee,
  Thou shouldst from my heart be as my true shadow.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn;
For love didst use this vow, as in it dooth live,
And thou from thence, full of new thoughts and fears,
Made it a vow to thee that thou shouldst die.
O, love, though the first with love forgot,
The second never gave me any excuse,
But went off and proved false to me more;
Though I thought myself twice guilty, yet thou knowsest
That that I had never done any wrong.
  And hence thou shalt live, though I once take thee from me.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
He slept, his love-brand gone, with beauty dead,
And with the golden robe stole away.
Then was this love made by old cunning,
By frequent incantations made old maid,
By tongue, perfumed with folly, by song
Which in youth strange rhyme with style dies,
By false rhyme of niggard rhyme with gold.
  But young lovers, fond of tongue and truth,
  Unfettered by poets in time of doom,
  By vow or rhyme eternal nightly stole away.
