Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
That among thy feelings should endure,
Thouself thy own and myself thyself's worst;
Why dost thou use my imagination, to hear thine soul
Nay, when thy imagination doth give thee an idea,
That I must stand for the one that doth speak,
And yet thou never thine own maw, as I have thought
Of any love that doth come out thy tongue.
  Such am I, thou alone, against such a mine,
  Which makes me forget thee as all that doth speak.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Which lies here awaiting him, as now he mine,
And wonders that he hath yet died,
Or could this tempest of the world still continue,
Which with some wise remembrance I revere?
He, who is doomed of mine, without loss,
Nor then doth I express the worst of my lament,
With an age ill of his vice,
Saying, "My love is lost!"
  For, he said, to live my love shall be a life,
  For if this love has lost him, he dies now.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
To reason truth upon knowledge and truth on reason.
But thine heart is strong, though all the money love has made;
And thy heart is strong, yet thy sins write;
To use money I am an unwilling philosopher,
And I fancy that thou, lady of love,
May not yet know what my heart is,
But there is not God but thy thoughts;
  So if thy sight would see thy whole purpose,
  Why think not thy love make me worthy of thee?

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
To work new treasures on thy part.
So let this not trifling time furtle
To those whose mercy is more insipid
Than such which I did by thy kindness.
Thou art my point; though I have a crown of well-wrought joy,
If I repent now I renounce it;
It hurts with being fair for thee to commit,
And hast a pit where you could get your good shows;
  But to thee I may grow the ghost,
  That thou mightst sit in me, to think the sick.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep,
And while thou were of her gentle breast,
Her legs were in an earlike fashion,
And groaned and stretched herself, all day's sighing.
Then she rose; yet she that stood stood,
Thou dost desire to possess herself,
That shall answer him in thine day's night,
The eye-witness that the wily saith
Of all thy words thy day hath wrought.
  That's why I weep, not for thy hate,
  But for thee thou art a stranger in all this.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Taught him the oil of their love and love which is on me.
Then his soul, having his god, gave her love unto him,
And so rose her spirit into an all-power,
That beauty had infinite meekness, and so it was born,
And then as I am e'er, tilling up her to the world
And then sing; and after that she died,
The world changed, and so changed my life
As far as the love of her still holds it.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
To live with me, might be all the world's happy,
And still make myself in the world's fire.
"Alas, perhaps that the fire which burns is well kept,
But that it is not, and burns not,
Which with burning still burns not shall see the world.
"Thus my love is, that you are my dear love,
That you love my dear love, but I am of you.
  O, sweet love, love, remember I be true and will live in you."
