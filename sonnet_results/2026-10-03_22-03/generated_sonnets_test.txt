Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
The frowning brow of that sweet babe I love,
Doth cover that rose of mine to kiss;
I shan't bear her slander, for I love her in all thy praise.
And yet, ever wary of such odious behavior,
I muse: "I love your sweet, sweet self,"
To which my love answers, "I love thee for whom thou breathe'st."
  Mine, too, is his, for the word his is sweet.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
As if in our shadow were thou, a face?
Nay, dost thou fall into my sight,
Shall I lose my thoughts by going to sleep,
Though I feel that I still live with thee when thou strangle'st,
Or do I think of thee when thou shalt leap,
For thou that dost seem the fairest do,
The sum of my sins, and thee alone.
  In love, therefore, I find no difference,
  Because thou that dost seem to live with me when I mourn.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Flattered to the wind in the rain,
Lailed the prime of his love, but none so;
Each sighing in him thorns the heart;
The love of his own body, his love in the rain,
Called the shadow of his air
And made nothing above it.
All present buried, all woe to the world,
O, that nothing to the world may be seen
Of true love's love's true form,
As the heart of a child, as the shadow of his own breath.
  Or perhaps none but his true love's name still remains.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
To say, 'My love, despite my love's fierce folly,
How my love did deceive me, that, out of my heart
And his passion did break my spirit,
That I should think of him worthy,
Upon his true love's ill thought change.
Thus love, though it have deceived me,
What my love did deceive me was true,
That I thought him worth having, that I should show him his worth.
  But now I see that no lie lies
  But my love says so, that no doubting mind can see
  His love's sad ending.

11

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And farther than him to the heaven of thine eyes
Will not grieve that I should neglect thee,
And with him thy love be to my heart,
And no subject more active nor more stout
Than thou art, when he's not with thee;
And yet I love him not to death,
Nor, when thou denyest him, to my plea
I say I have not loved thee since the first acquaintance,
  To hear thee speak in need of me.
  Such is thy love, so shall my moan and sigh
  As thou art mine.
