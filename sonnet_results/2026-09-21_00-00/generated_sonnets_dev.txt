Thine eyes I love, and they, as pitying me,
Knowing thy heart torment me with disdain,
Have put on black, and loving mourners be,
And you to all sorrow say, "Husband, I love thee more;
Thou art better than any flower of my day."
What do I write, poor sad child, that shall be answered,
Unless, by being happy, I make thy grief depart
With such melancholy and such a sigh as to please
That mourners like myself may say, "Thou art happier than this,
Which all women hate to see in my sight."

Beshrew that heart that makes my heart to groan
For that deep wound it gives my friend and me.
Is 't not enough to torture me alone,
To die in the heat of love's agony,
And to that, to be buried with a friend that loves thee?
Is it not enough to die in my grief,
To lie in this earth to weep,
And for thy sake alone to breathe?
O, what gift of thy sweet love can there be
That I, doting on thy part, should yet live,
Or be buried with thee, to the end?
  If this were so, the world would be an ocean
  where the fresh-baked flowers alone dwell.

3

Whoever hath her wish, thou hast thy will,
And will to boot, and will in overplus.
More than enough am I that vex thee still,
Than more than thou yet know'st: this, that thou keep'st it a secret,
From all the world to myself and myself alone.
Thou shalt not read of my wont'st, but read in thy will
The reason why I this day do my will,
And after that keep the things in my power,
And things whatsoever they require I desire.
  Then wouldst thou be a sovereign, which I am not;
  For I, when I set purpose, am not the wise,
  But that which wills is.

If thy soul check thee that I come so near,
Swear to thy blind soul that I was thy will,
And will, thy soul knows, is admitted there.
If thou say so, thou surest prove true,
For 'tis not thy will that thou shouldst my time so,
Which, when thou gav'st, so soon wert created;
For true will bring with itself thy thoughts,
Which shall find thee when thou goest thither,
That thou shalt find an idol in my soul;
And when my muse calls me to come,
Thou might'st be my sovereign, and I no idol,
Unless by thy consent I be gone thither.
  So, if thou wilt swear, I will come thither.

Thou blind fool, Love, what dost thou to mine eyes
That they behold and see not what they see?
They know what beauty is, see where it lies,
And yet look not into thine own heart.
Herein lies the secret of what love is
Made manifest by thy own inward gaze,
Thy own heart being a mirror of that which thou dost see.
Do not make thyself behold the beauty of others;
For, being themselves to others thyself,
They can hate thee not, being themselves thyself.
O, not my love's hatred do I hate
But in another's heart that thine own is seen,
Which thou canst read in other men's eyes.
  But in thy own heart behold not the true face

When my love swears that she is made of truth
I do believe her though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Thy love's store-room to lie is full of gold,
And her no silver to pay me dear.
Yet, if I did lose that which thou dost take,
O, so ill would I be, and yet there's no better
Than to lose my love and the first thing I do best,
The heart of thine inward truth.
O, give me thy soul and love's treasure to spend,
The poor mortal thing will live and be more happy.
  For my love's sake, where's my love's treasure?
  Thy sweet heart belongs to the grave

Be wise as thou art cruel; do not press
My tongue-tied patience with too much disdain,
Lest sorrow lend me words, and words express thy thoughts.
O, say thou now, when I speak of thine end
Of sorrow to some friend or chance,
Who shall blame me if I do not praise thee so
When, by the abuse of truth, I wear out this woe
And give others the blame; now is the time,
When, to the old world, I chide those who have lost
And others the thanks of love.
  Be thou an eternal remedy, then,
  For all to suffer is better than to be born anew.

9

In faith, I do not love thee with mine eyes,
For they in thee a thousand errors note;
But 'tis my heart that loves what they despise,
And inward eyes seldom find faults that he may praise.
Love, in me, has all her outward parts stamped
And with their bases hid is the true heart.
For to thee is my lover all my substance made,
And all my clay, all my goodness, all my comfort,
Thou art all the substance that I in thee.
  For though all that is thine be false, yet in me
  All is true.

10

Love is my sin, and thy dear virtue hate,
Hate of my sin, grounded on sinful loving.
O, but with mine compare thou thine own state,
To whom my love is subject, and my respect
To thyself in whose praise thou dost stand.
By nature, therefore, I love thee for thy beauty,
And thou art the 't for my love's sake,
The true root of my pride, which by thee dost grow.
  But, scorn, thou must needs hate,
  For I love thee to so great an extent,
  That I hate not to see thee' fall.

11

Lo, as a careful huswife runs to catch
One of her feathered creatures broke away,
Sets down her babe, and makes all swift dispatch,
Though for the cause her wound should be mortal.
No, where is that pity, where is that sweet-tempered spirit,
To besiege that womb which should dispense?
O, that wretch, that used to breed,
Who not with vigor nor skill dooth his work,
Can his starving babe die, though the proudest boast
Be uttered by those who rank him with the best.
  Therefore, when a child with youth spoils,
  The youth kills, but the young that died was his chief foe.
