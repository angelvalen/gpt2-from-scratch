Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
That my sweet creature should die unprovided
For my love's end of all love's use?
O, be patient, my love, and grant my plea;
Grant it, that I with thy love can live,
And use myself to the best of my wits.
Be willing that I grant what I think best.
O, the contented slave is contented,
Whilst the proud tyrant is tyrant.
  So I, whose sweet pleasure was never in a rage,
  Give all my souls to thee and suffer not.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Feeding on that which doth renew health.
Such are my loves, such are my failings,
My loves that from days long dead return again;
Such are my days that I sleep happy,
And my heart doth love itself all in such wise,
That it may yet be in that state of health,
That never I nor I alone love is dead,
  But this love and that which doth love the same.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
That with their maladies looks and eyes
Gild, by those? in my heart I see no defect,
Although dark, long-fingered, fair, or fair's beauty
Consumed with glass, which all my love is;
Hence, if I lie not to my eyes,
My black discontent and my fair discontent
Are two against one; I am one to love,
And to love only the parts that bear upon my heart.
  This is the sum of my love, that to love one
  I best hate; therefore I hate none but myself.

Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot thee,
And for that neglect I suffer,
And then my love is in a false semblance made?
I love thee, for that I love thee I tell,
And so thy quiet sake doth give my heart a pleasure.
And since my love am still confined to thee,
It seems of itself not to neglect thee,
Nor to say of myself that love doth decrease.
  To love is a precious delight in this world,
  That other things are more happy than that which is.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Sincerity of heart, joy of mind,
Even to love when weak, when in another's sight.
Sweet loving is not the strange strange mark,
That 'tis made of more than it appears;
It is, in truth, of this alone that I write
And that as one may say, 'tis so:
Love's meaning is simple and self-evident.
  Be content, dear sweet love, to know
  That 't is all sweet and true when heart, mind, tongue, and brain
  Are in accord.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn;
And yet for thy love I yield myself part;
Although in me thou hast part, yet in thee I'll part.
O, take heed of that, lest I strangle thee,
Making thee a true half-beloved of this world,
And in thy married state make a widow.
  Even so, though thou give me my love, yet I'll give thee this:
  If thou art married, I will give thee no thing more;
  But even then, to love I'll give thee this sum: to live thou must.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep
In shame his cheek, with all the beauties that yellow.
So for shame he slept, his shame and beauty bright,
And in the morning star and hours reigned.
The fair beauty did reproach him more than all his age;
And he dreamed that, being gay,
Cupid had seen his beauty wrought.
He would have made love, and was sorry
Of having gone but on so sweet a night,
But fortune taught him to be a fool.
  So long as love lives, that fair beauty,
  As fair as her beauty appears, suffers no change.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep
Drew for their dear love and look on this state.
With plump flowers blooming in thorns,
Like beasts and ravens they cast their light on
And then to every eye, one, another, all
He shows the rude beauty of the selfsame,
Which in fair compare would be an idol to the world.
The throng of such witnesses gives place
To such as love and only love, and lives for their sight
And their table, their hair and their beauty is shown
  To such as in thy eyes were dumb doth excel.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Delivering falsehoods of my will;
Such is the love of truth, like a breast,
Which answers truth with truth, and hath the least part,
Which none but faith can bear to the full.
Mine eyes, if I love true, see her lies;
But mine ears, if I love her falsehoods, hear them.
  Mine ears never with my love have strayed from truth.
  She that tells me a falsehood is my truth.
